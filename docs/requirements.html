Purpose & first release

Reduce the effort of deciding what to do next while preserving a reliable project and client history. The first release must bring the daily planner and project meetings/follow-ups together.

  • Home screen combines a suggested day, personal to-dos, and an overview of projects needing attention.
  • Designed for your work computer. Installable PWA; only you log in.
  • You manually enter meetings, tasks, priorities, duration estimates, and deadlines.
  • Quiet reminders inside the app. No sounds or desktop notifications.
  • No Outlook, calendar, Teams, or email account connection. Teams content is pasted; emails are drafts you send yourself.
  • No budget or fee tracking. No enforced task dependencies.

This document describes the agreed product. The interaction below uses sample data; it is not a working application or an external integration.

Daily planner

  • Day view resembling Outlook: time blocks and a moving current-time line.
  • Normal hours: Monday–Friday, 8:45am–6pm, Asia/Singapore. Anything outside this window, including weekends, is overtime. No automatic lunch or break reservation.
  • Open with a suggested schedule. Prioritise overdue follow-ups, then consider your priorities, deadlines, and estimated durations.
  • Client meetings keep their scheduled times. Task blocks can automatically move or split around meetings as the day changes.
  • Drag and drop tasks to change timing or order. Manually placed blocks stay pinned during automatic replanning until you move or release them.
  • Moving a schedule block does not change the underlying deadline.
  • If work does not fit normal hours, let you choose overtime or moving tasks to another working day; flag deadline risk.
  • Estimates are planning guides. Running over does not prompt for a new estimate.
  • Support recurring meetings and tasks; edits can apply to one occurrence or the series.
  • Personal tasks can exist without a project.

Timer and meeting overlap

You control Start, Pause, and Finish. If a meeting begins while a task timer is running, show “Time overlap: Pause task or Continue.” Never pause automatically. Continue preserves both records, while overlapping minutes count once in total working time.

Acceptance examples
  • A running task passes its estimate: other unpinned tasks can shift, but a 2pm client call stays at 2pm.
  • You drag a task to 4pm: replanning preserves its position and original due date.
  • A task would end at 7pm: present overtime versus deferral for your choice.

Clients & projects

Separate client and project records with a many-to-many relationship: one client can have many projects, and one project can involve many clients.

  • Project overview combines milestones, deadlines, status, blockers, team delivery, upcoming meetings, client actions, and PBC requests.
  • Each project has a full one-page view of pending and completed work.
  • Client information and contacts live in client records. Individual meetings/actions/PBC items do not require client-level tagging for now.
  • Store engagement letters, FSDs, and other supporting documents with version history.
  • Link relevant document versions to meetings and scope decisions; preserve earlier versions.
  • Record blockers without coupling tasks through dependency rules.

Singapore & Hanoi team visibility

See what each team is doing and whether delivery is slow, particularly when deadlines are missed. You maintain the records from project-specific Teams channels.

  • Paste weekly plans separately into each project.
  • Paste daily updates covering yesterday’s progress, today’s plan, and blockers/issues. Hanoi normally updates at 11am Singapore time.
  • Store the original message, message date, summary, and reviewed changes. Track how progress changes over time.
  • AI proposes task matching, new tasks, statuses, deadlines, assignees, and blockers for review.
  • Flag missing deadlines. Daily updates do not require hours and must not flag missing hours.
  • Allow estimated task hours when the team begins providing them; do not invent actual time from narrative updates.
  • Flag missed deadlines, work remaining open beyond one week, and apparent lack of progress across updates.
  • GitLab/Jira versus Teams discrepancies are visible for that day and retained in history. Do not carry the same alert across days; fresh conflicting evidence can create a new flag.

To refine during implementation: whether “beyond a week” means seven calendar days or the weekly planning cycle, and when the age starts.

Meetings, minutes & documents

  1. Create a meeting within a project with date/time, attendees, and purpose.
  2. Type structured notes in the app, or download a prefilled Word template.
  3. Template includes outstanding project actions and PBC items, plus discussion, decisions, scope changes, actions, document requests, and screenshot space.
  4. Upload the completed Word document. Preserve its original content and embedded images as evidence.
  5. AI drafts minutes, summaries, proposed actions, PBC requests, scope changes, and an email.
  6. You review and edit suggestions before accepting them into project records. You send any email or document yourself.

Screenshots remain supporting evidence. They are not automatically interpreted or summarised.

Scope accountability

Keep a dated history of discussed changes, their source meeting and FSD version, and whether the client explicitly confirmed them. An unconfirmed change generates a follow-up item to seek confirmation. Do not present discussion alone as client approval.

Actions, follow-ups & PBC requests

PBC means “Prepared by client”: requested documents or clarifications. Track responsible contact/owner, deadline, notes, status, and next follow-up date at project level.

  • You choose the next follow-up date. Overdue items stay visible until resolved or rescheduled.
  • Dated activity history records follow-up attempts and responses, such as “Email sent; client has not replied.”
  • Sending a follow-up does not close the underlying item. Set another follow-up date while still awaiting a reply.
  • Every deadline change preserves the old date, new date, time of change, and mandatory reason.

Opportunity lifecycle

Contact → Schedule meeting → Call → Walkthrough → Proposal → Proposal follow-up

  • Actions and follow-ups at every stage.
  • Follow-up emails become small tasks; calls and walkthroughs become fixed meetings.
  • When confirmed, convert an opportunity into a project while preserving meetings, proposals, notes, and follow-up history.

Time & reporting

  • Report this week, this month, custom ranges, and all time.
  • Break down time by project, meeting versus task, and normal hours versus overtime; show standalone tasks separately.
  • Task time comes from Start/Pause/Finish sessions. Meetings initially count scheduled duration, which you can correct.
  • Count the union of overlapping intervals once in overall working-time totals. Preserve each activity’s duration for drill-down.
  • Clearly distinguish activity durations from deduplicated totals; do not add overlapping category totals and call them total working time.
  • Show estimates versus actuals over time to help you learn realistic task durations.
  • Do not treat pasted team updates as measured team hours.

Connectors & AI

Read-only work-board connectors

  • Separate GitLab and Jira implementations behind a shared connector interface.
  • Multiple GitLab repositories and Jira boards can map to one project.
  • Pull available tasks/issues, source links, assignees, statuses, and deadlines.
  • Never write changes back to GitLab or Jira.
  • Imported issues are project visibility/FYI. They enter your personal planner only when you create a personal task.
  • Retain source IDs, source timestamps, last sync time, and sync failures so stale data is visible.

AI-provider connectors

  • Ollama is the default, using your on-prem endpoint.
  • Cloud AI connector uses the same application interface and runs only when explicitly selected.
  • No automatic fallback from Ollama to cloud.
  • Used for Teams summaries, meeting minutes, proposed actions, scope extraction, and draft emails.
  • AI output is a reviewable draft. A human accepts updates into official records.
  • Preserve source evidence and link accepted outputs to it.

Data & proposed architecture

The following is an implementation proposal supporting the agreed requirements; the software stack is not yet selected.

Core records
  • Clients, client contacts, projects, and client–project links.
  • Opportunities, stages, proposals, and conversion history.
  • Tasks/actions/PBC items, deadlines, follow-up dates, statuses, activities, and deadline-change history.
  • Calendar meetings, recurrence series, schedule blocks, manual pin state, and time sessions.
  • Teams source updates, weekly plans, extracted draft changes, and progress snapshots.
  • Meeting documents, attachments, document versions, minutes drafts, decisions, and scope confirmations.
  • External connections, project source mappings, imported issues, sync runs, and dated discrepancies.
  • AI-provider settings and review/acceptance records.
Suggested module boundaries

planner, time-tracking, clients-projects, team-updates, meetings-documents, actions-followups, opportunities, reporting, connectors/gitlab, connectors/jira, ai/ollama, ai/cloud, and audit-history.

UI and domain logic depend on shared interfaces, keeping vendor-specific authentication, APIs, field mappings, and response handling inside adapters.

Hosting & operations

Self-host on the MacBook or a company machine, potentially the Dell DG10. The host must run continuously for remote availability. Include single-user authentication, HTTPS for remote PWA use, database/attachment backups, and secure connector credentials.

Offline behaviour remains to be decided. A PWA installation does not by itself guarantee offline editing, AI access, or board syncing.

Remaining implementation choices
  • Cloud AI provider, model choices, and Ollama endpoint configuration.
  • GitLab/Jira instance URLs, access permissions, authentication, field mapping, and sync cadence.
  • How unfinished tasks are treated after a manually pinned time has passed.
  • How blocked tasks enter suggested schedules without enforced dependencies.
  • Overtime limits and recurrence exception behaviour.
  • Opportunity won/lost/on-hold states, project archive rules, and missed/cancelled meetings.
  • Offline scope, backup destination, deployment details, and document size limits.
